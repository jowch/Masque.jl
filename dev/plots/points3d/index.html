Points and lines on an Axis3

On an Axis3, masque(fig) makes a scatter!, meshscatter! and lines! respond as they do in 2D, with z added: hovering a marker shows its x, y and z, and a click selects it and sends it to your notebook. Hover the markers below, then click one:

Notebook as text
using Masque, CairoMakie, Random
begin
    rng = Xoshiro(21)
    x = randn(rng, 40)
    y = randn(rng, 40)
    z = 0.6 .* x .+ 0.3 .* y .+ 0.4 .* randn(rng, 40)

    fig = Figure(size = (520, 380))
    ax = Axis3(fig[1, 1]; xlabel = "Dose", ylabel = "Age", zlabel = "Response")
    sc = scatter!(ax, x, y, z; markersize = 10)
    nothing
end
@bind pick masque(fig; bind = sc)

pick

What a click returns

A click gives an ElementEvent with these fields. Here the figure passes the scatter as bind, so pick is that event itself; with @bind sel masque(fig), it is sel.scatter.

FieldWhat it holds
indexthe marker's place in your data
x, y, zthe point you plotted

Show your own data

Give each marker a payload, one per marker in the order of your data. The template can use x, y and z next to your fields:

Notebook as text
masque(fig, interactables(sc; payloads = [(patient = "P$(i)",) for i in 1:40], tooltip = masque"<b>$(patient)</b><br>response $(z:.2f)"))

Hover lines and spheres

A lines! responds along its whole length, with the point you plotted nearest the pointer, and a meshscatter! responds over each sphere, the one nearer the camera where two overlap:

Notebook as text
begin
    t = range(0, 4pi; length = 120)
    fig2 = Figure(size = (520, 380))
    ax2 = Axis3(fig2[1, 1])
    lines!(ax2, cos.(t), sin.(t), t ./ 4pi; linewidth = 2)
    meshscatter!(ax2, cos.(t[1:20:end]), sin.(t[1:20:end]), t[1:20:end] ./ 4pi; markersize = 0.06, color = :orange)
    masque(fig2)
end

Turn the view

The figures on this page stay put, because turning one has Julia draw it again. In your notebook, Explore a 3D figure shows how to orbit the view by dragging.

Supported plots

On an Axis3, scatter!, meshscatter!, lines!, linesegments!, scatterlines!, text!, surface!, wireframe! and arrows3d! respond. A scatter! sized in data units (markerspace = :data) with a marker_offset is skipped with a warning, and so is any other plot, with a note that names it.

Where to go next

For a surface, see surface!; for arrows, arrows3d!. The 2D pages, scatter!, meshscatter! and lines!, cover the rest of what each plot can do.
