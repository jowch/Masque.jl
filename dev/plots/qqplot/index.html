qqplot! and qqnorm!

With a qqline, a qqplot! or qqnorm! plot has two parts, and masque(fig) makes each respond on its own: the points and the reference line. Without one, the plot has only the points. Hovering a point shows its two quantiles, and a click selects it and sends it to your notebook. Hover the points below, then click one:

Notebook as text
using Masque, CairoMakie, Random
begin
    rng = Xoshiro(6)
    wait = round.(randexp(rng, 80) .* 5; digits = 1)

    fig = Figure(size = (520, 320))
    ax = Axis(fig[1, 1]; xlabel = "Normal quantile", ylabel = "Wait (min)")
    q = qqnorm!(ax, wait; qqline = :fitrobust, markersize = 6)
    nothing
end
@bind sel masque(fig)

sel

What a click returns

Each part has its own field in the @bind value: sel.scatter for the points and sel.segments for the line, or scatter_2 and so on when the figure already has a part of that name. A click on a point gives an ElementEvent with these fields:

FieldWhat it holds
indexthe point's place from the smallest quantile up, so 1 is the lowest point
xthe quantile of the normal distribution, or of your first sample for qqplot!
ythe quantile of your data

The points come smallest first, not in the order of your data.

Show your own data

To say which observation a point is, give the points a payload each, under the part's name, scatter. Because the points come smallest first, sort your rows the same way: sortperm(wait) lists the customers from the shortest wait up. (A qqplot! of two samples of different lengths draws as many points as the shorter sample has, so give it that many payloads.) A tooltip template, keyed by the same part name, can use the payload's fields next to x and y; the line keeps its default tooltip:

Notebook as text
customer = sortperm(wait)
masque(
    fig,
    interactables(
        q;
        payloads = (
            scatter = [(customer = c,) for c in customer],
        ),
        tooltip = (
            scatter = masque"<b>Customer $(customer)</b><br>waited $(y) min",
        ),
    ),
)

A click on a point also returns its payload, so sel.scatter.customer is the customer you clicked. Plots with several parts says how payloads and tooltips work for each part.

Supported axes

qqplot! and qqnorm! are drawn as points and a line, so they respond on a 2D Axis, an Axis3 and a PolarAxis, as scatter! and linesegments! do.
